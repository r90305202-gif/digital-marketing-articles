# 5 Android Location Settings You Should Check Today

### A Practical Guide to Reviewing App Permissions, Location Sharing, and Google Maps Timeline

## Introduction

Your Android phone uses location information to provide useful services. Google Maps uses it for navigation. Weather applications use it to show local forecasts. Delivery applications use it to help drivers find the correct address.

However, not every application needs access to your location.

Some apps need your location only while you use them. Others may request access in the background. Certain applications need your precise location, while others can work with an approximate location.

Android provides several settings that let you review and control these permissions.

In this guide, we will examine five important Android location settings:

1. App location permissions.
2. Precise and approximate location.
3. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scanning.
4. Google Location Sharing.
5. Google Maps Timeline.

You will learn what each setting does, where to find it, and how to make sensible privacy choices.

**Important:** Menu names and available options vary by Android version and phone manufacturer. The steps below describe common Android settings. Your Samsung, OnePlus, Xiaomi, Motorola, or Pixel phone may use slightly different labels.

## 1. Review App Location Permissions

The first step is to check which applications can access your phone's location.

An application may request location access for a genuine reason. For example:

- Navigation apps need location information to provide directions.
- Weather apps may use location to show local forecasts.
- Ride-booking apps need location to identify pickup points.
- Camera apps may use location to add location information to photos.
- Shopping apps may use location to display nearby stores.

However, some applications may not need location access for their main functions.

Reviewing permissions helps you decide which apps should have access.

### How to check app location permissions

Follow these steps:

1. Open your phone's **Settings**.
2. Select **Location**.
3. Tap **App location permissions**.
4. Review the applications listed on the screen.
5. Select an application to inspect its permission.
6. Change the permission if the application does not need its current level of access.

Depending on your Android version, you may see options such as:

| Permission | What it means |
|---|---|
| Allow all the time | The app can access location in the background, subject to Android restrictions. |
| Allow only while using the app | The app can access location while you use it. |
| Ask every time | Android asks before granting access for a session. |
| Don't allow | The app cannot access device location through that permission. |

### Which permission should you choose?

For many ordinary applications, **Allow only while using the app** is a sensible starting point.

For an application that does not need location, choose **Don't allow**.

Some applications need background location for features that must work when the app is not open. Review those requests carefully rather than automatically approving or denying them.

### A useful privacy habit

Check your location permissions every few months.

Also review permissions after installing unfamiliar applications or when an app requests access that does not seem necessary.

**Remember:** Background location permission does not automatically prove that an application is misusing your information. It means the application has permission to access location under the conditions allowed by Android.

## 2. Control Precise and Approximate Location

Android allows users to control how accurately an application can access their location.

This is an important privacy option.

### What is precise location?

Precise location allows an application to access a more exact estimate of your device's location.

This can help applications that need accurate positioning, including:

- Turn-by-turn navigation.
- Ride-booking services.
- Emergency-related applications.
- Location-based check-in services.

### What is approximate location?

Approximate location provides a general area rather than an exact position.

It may be sufficient for applications that only need to know your general area.

For example, a weather application may not need your exact street address to display a local forecast.

### How to change precise location access

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Location**.
3. Select **App location permissions**.
4. Choose an application.
5. Look for **Use precise location**.
6. Turn it off when approximate location is sufficient and the option is available.

You can keep precise location enabled for applications that genuinely need accurate positioning.

### Precise location versus Location Accuracy

These two settings are different.

- **Precise location** controls the location accuracy available to an individual application.
- **Location Accuracy** is a device-level service that can use available signals to improve location estimates.

Turning off Location Accuracy may affect services such as Google Maps and device-finding features. It is not a replacement for reviewing individual app permissions.

**Practical advice:** Use approximate location when it meets the application's needs. Keep precise location available for services that depend on accurate positioning.

## 3. Review Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Scanning

Your Android phone can scan for nearby Wi-Fi access points and Bluetooth devices to help improve location estimates.

These scanning options are separate from simply connecting to a Wi-Fi network or pairing a Bluetooth accessory.

### What does Wi-Fi scanning do?

Wi-Fi scanning allows your phone to detect nearby Wi-Fi access points.

Android can use available wireless information to help estimate your location, especially in places where GPS signals are weak.

### What does Bluetooth scanning do?

Bluetooth scanning allows your phone to detect nearby Bluetooth devices.

This information can help location-related services improve their estimates or support certain nearby-device features.

### How to find these settings

**Android 12 and later:**

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Select **Location**.
3. Tap **Location Services**.
4. Find **Wi-Fi scanning**.
5. Find **Bluetooth scanning**.
6. Review both options.

**Android 11 and earlier:**

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Select **Location**.
3. Look for **Wi-Fi scanning** and **Bluetooth scanning**.

The exact menu may differ by manufacturer.

### Should you turn them off?

That depends on your needs.

If you do not use services that benefit from additional scanning, you can review whether these options are necessary.

However, turning them off may reduce location accuracy or affect some device features.

Do not assume that disabling scanning makes your phone completely invisible. Other location services and network functions may still operate.

**Practical advice:** Understand what each scanning option does before changing it. Keep features enabled when you rely on them.

## 4. Check Google Location Sharing

Google Location Sharing lets you share your real-time location with selected people through supported Google services, including Google Maps.

This feature can be useful when:

- Meeting family members.
- Coordinating a journey.
- Letting someone know when you may arrive.
- Sharing your location during a trip.

However, you should know exactly who can see your location.

### How to review Location Sharing

1. Open **Google Maps**.
2. Tap your profile picture or initial.
3. Select **Location sharing**.
4. Review the people listed.
5. Check whether you still want to share your location with each person.

If you no longer want to share your location with someone, select that person's entry and choose the option to stop sharing.

On supported Android devices, you can also review:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Select **Location**.
3. Tap **Location Services**.
4. Open **Google Location Sharing**.
5. Review the available sharing controls.

### An important distinction

Changing an application's location permission does not automatically stop Google Location Sharing.

For example, changing Google Maps' app permission is not the same as stopping a location share with another person.

Google provides separate controls for Location Sharing. Review those controls directly.

### Make a habit of reviewing shared locations

Check your sharing list periodically.

Ask yourself:

- Do I recognise everyone listed?
- Do I still need to share my location with them?
- Is the sharing arrangement still relevant?
- Do I understand which Google account is being used?

Only share your location with people you trust.

## 5. Review Google Maps Timeline

Google Maps Timeline is a feature that can help you review places and routes associated with your Google Maps activity.

When enabled, Timeline can save visits and routes to your device. Its availability and backup behaviour depend on your account, device, and current Google Maps settings.

This feature can be useful for remembering journeys. However, it is worth reviewing if you do not want a location history.

### How to check Timeline

1. Open **Google Maps**.
2. Tap your profile picture or initial.
3. Select **Your Timeline**.
4. Open the Timeline settings or **Location & privacy settings**.
5. Review whether Timeline is enabled.
6. Check the available backup and auto-delete options.

The exact menu layout may change as Google updates Maps.

### What can you control?

Depending on your current settings and device, Google Maps may provide options to:

- Turn Timeline off.
- Delete Timeline activity.
- Set automatic deletion.
- Review or edit saved visits.
- Manage Timeline backups.

Google currently offers automatic deletion periods such as 3, 18, or 36 months in supported Timeline settings.

Read the confirmation screen carefully before deleting information. Some deletion actions may be permanent.

### Timeline and Location Sharing are different

This distinction matters.

**Timeline** helps you review saved visits and routes.

**Location Sharing** lets selected people see your shared location.

Turning off Timeline does not automatically stop Location Sharing. Review both features separately.

## Additional Privacy Tips for Android Users

The five settings above are a useful starting point. You can also follow these everyday practices.

### 1. Install applications from trusted sources

Check the application developer, reviews, requested permissions, and privacy information before installing an app.

### 2. Keep Android updated

Install security updates and operating system updates when your device makes them available.

### 3. Review unused applications

Remove applications you no longer need. An application you do not use may still have permissions until you uninstall it or change those permissions.

### 4. Use strong account security

Use a strong, unique password for your Google Account. Enable two-step verification where available.

### 5. Check your Google Account activity

Review the privacy and activity controls associated with your Google Account. Understand which services save activity and what controls are available.

### 6. Be careful when sharing your location

Before sharing your real-time location, check the recipient and sharing duration.

### 7. Avoid unnecessary permission requests

If an application asks for location access when the feature does not appear to need it, review the request before approving it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Does turning off location permission stop all tracking?

No. It prevents an app from accessing device location through that permission. Other services may have separate settings, and websites or networks may infer a general location from other information.

### 2. Is approximate location safer than precise location?

Approximate location gives an app less exact location information. It can be a good choice when the app does not need your exact position. It does not guarantee complete privacy.

### 3. Does turning off Wi-Fi scanning disconnect my phone from Wi-Fi?

No. Wi-Fi scanning and connecting to a Wi-Fi network are different functions. Turning off scanning does not normally disable your Wi-Fi connection.

### 4. Does turning off Bluetooth scanning disable Bluetooth?

No. Bluetooth scanning is separate from the main Bluetooth connection control. Your device may still connect to paired Bluetooth accessories.

### 5. Does disabling Timeline stop Google Location Sharing?

No. Timeline and Location Sharing are separate features. Review each one independently.

### 6. Will these changes stop all location collection?

No. These settings control specific Android and Google features. They do not guarantee that every application, website, network, or service will stop receiving all location-related information.

### 7. Do all Android phones have the same settings?

No. Android versions and manufacturers can change menu names, locations, and available controls. If a setting is missing, use your phone's Settings search feature.
